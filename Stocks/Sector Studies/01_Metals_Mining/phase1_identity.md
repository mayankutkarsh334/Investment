# Phase 1 — Sector Identity & Structure: Metals & Mining

## Scope

This study covers **base metals and mining** — steel, aluminium, copper, zinc, and ore mining. It **excludes Gold & Precious Metals** (Titan, Kalyan, Muthoot, Manappuram, Senco, PC Jeweller), which will be studied as a separate sector due to fundamentally different business models (B2C retail / NBFC), counter-cyclical behavior, and low beta.

---

## 1.1 — Sector Overview

**What the sector does:** Extracts, processes, smelts, and sells metals and minerals — from raw ore mining through smelting/refining to finished metal products.

**GDP contribution:** Mining contributes ~1.8% of India's GDP (target: 2.5% by FY27). The broader metals value chain (including steel manufacturing) contributes ~10-11% of industrial GDP.

**Market size:**
- Steel market: ~$150 billion (2026), projected to reach $227 billion by 2032 (CAGR 7.1%)
- Aluminium market: production capacity ~4.1 MTPA, expanding by 2 MTPA over 5 years
- Copper: domestic consumption ~750,000 tonnes vs production ~555,000 tonnes (structural import dependence)
- Mineral production value: ₹92,846 crore in H1 FY26 (+16.5% YoY vs H1 FY25)

**India's global position:**
- World's **2nd largest crude steel producer** (164 MT in FY25) — only top-10 nation with double-digit growth
- World's **5th largest bauxite reserves** (aluminium feedstock)
- World's **2nd largest integrated zinc producer** (Hindustan Zinc)
- Structural **copper deficit** — imports 90-95% of copper concentrate

## 1.2 — Sub-Group Structure

The sector is split into **3 sub-groups** for study. Each has distinct business models, drivers, and risk profiles.

### Sub-Group A — Steel

**Revenue model:** Sell finished steel (hot-rolled, cold-rolled, coated, stainless) at spot/contract prices. Margin = steel realization price minus (iron ore + coking coal + power + conversion cost). Margins are highly cyclical.

**Industry structure:**
- Moderately consolidated — top 3 players hold ~45% market share
- ~50 companies operating, but dominated by 5–6 large integrated players
- BF-BOF route holds 46% share (large integrated plants); EAF and induction furnaces make up the rest

**Key players:**

| Company | Capacity (MTPA) | FY26 Production | Market Cap | Expansion Target |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|------------|-----------------|
| JSW Steel | 31.9 → 37 by mid-2026 | — | ~$30 billion (world's most valuable steel co) | 48.8 MTPA by 2030 |
| Tata Steel | 21.6 (India) + ~13 (global) | 23.48 MT (record, +8% YoY) | — | 40 MTPA by 2030 (India) |
| SAIL | ~20 | — | — | Modernization ongoing |
| JSPL | ~10 | 9.25 MT (record, +14% YoY) | — | Doubling structural steel to 2.4 MTPA by mid-2028 |
| Jindal Stainless | ~2.5 (stainless) | — | — | Stainless steel leader |
| APL Apollo | — | — | — | Structural tubes / value-added |

**End-use breakdown:** Construction & infrastructure (63%), automotive, capital goods, consumer durables

**Domestic vs export:** ~85% domestic / ~15% export

**Capital intensity:** Very high — ₹50,000-70,000 crore for a greenfield integrated steel plant

**Customer type:** B2B (construction companies, auto OEMs, infra contractors)

---

### Sub-Group B — Non-Ferrous Metals (Aluminium, Copper, Zinc)

**Revenue model:** Sell metal at LME-linked prices. Margins depend on input costs (power for aluminium, treatment charges for copper, mining cost for zinc).

**Industry structure:**
- **Aluminium:** Highly concentrated — 3 players control nearly all Indian production. India produces ~4.1 MTPA. Odisha alone contributes 54% of output.
- **Copper:** Effective duopoly — Hindalco (Birla Copper) smelts, Hindustan Copper mines. Sterlite Tuticorin (Vedanta) closed since 2018 on environmental grounds.
- **Zinc:** Near-monopoly — Hindustan Zinc has ~75% domestic market share.

**Key players:**

| Company | Metal | Role | Scale |
|---------|-------|------|-------|
| Hindalco Industries | Aluminium + Copper | Largest aluminium producer (1.3 MTPA smelting); largest copper smelter (500K tonne at Dahej — world's 6th largest); global presence via Novelis (aluminium rolling) | Diversified metals giant |
| Vedanta Ltd | Aluminium + Zinc (via HZL) + Oil & Gas | Largest aluminium capacity (2.4 MTPA via Jharsuguda + BALCO); controls Hindustan Zinc | Conglomerate, Anil Agarwal-promoted |
| NALCO | Aluminium | PSU; integrated bauxite mining + alumina refining + smelting | Q1 FY26 profit +77% YoY |
| Hindustan Copper (HCL) | Copper | India's sole copper ore miner; holds 40% of India's copper reserves | PSU; targeting 12 MTPA ore by FY31 |
| Hindustan Zinc (HZL) | Zinc + Silver | World's 2nd largest integrated zinc producer; 3rd largest silver producer | ~75% India zinc market |

**Key insight — monopoly/oligopoly pricing power:** Unlike steel (fragmented, import-exposed), non-ferrous sub-sectors have structural supply concentration. Hindustan Zinc's 75% market share and Hindalco's 50%+ copper smelting share give them pricing power that commodity producers rarely have. These stocks should score higher on fundamentals and have lower competitive risk.

**Domestic vs export:** Aluminium ~50-60% domestic / 40% export (Hindalco global via Novelis); Copper ~95% domestic; Zinc mostly domestic

**Capital intensity:** Very high (aluminium is extremely power-intensive; copper smelting is capital-heavy)

**Customer type:** B2B (auto, packaging, power cables, construction)

---

### Sub-Group C — Mining (Ore)

**Revenue model:** Extract and sell raw ore/minerals to downstream smelters and manufacturers at auction or contract prices. Near-pure commodity business. Margins depend on ore grade, extraction cost, and global commodity prices.

**Industry structure:**
- PSU-dominated — NMDC and MOIL are government-owned
- Derived demand business — iron ore demand follows steel production; manganese follows steel alloy production
- Regulated sector — mining leases granted by state/central government

**Key players:**

| Company | Mineral | Market Position |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| NMDC | Iron ore | India's largest producer and exporter; 45+ MTPA from 3 mechanized mines (Chhattisgarh + Karnataka); TTM revenue ~$3.18 billion |
| MOIL | Manganese ore | India's largest producer; ~53% domestic market share; sole producer of Electrolytic Manganese Dioxide (EMD); targeting 3.5 MTPA by 2030 |

**Domestic vs export:** ~90% domestic (sell to Indian steel/alloy plants)

**Capital intensity:** High (mining equipment, land acquisition, environmental clearances)

**Customer type:** B2B (steel mills, ferro-alloy producers)

**Key insight:** Mining stocks are a leveraged play on steel/metal demand. When steel demand rises, ore demand and prices follow. But they carry additional regulatory risk (mining bans, environmental clearances, royalty changes).

## 1.3 — Key Drivers

### Macro Drivers

| Driver | Current State (May 2026) | Impact on Sector |
|--------|-------------------------|-----------------|
| **India infrastructure capex** | Record ₹11.2 trillion in FY26 (+10% YoY); PM GatiShakti + railways | Direct demand for steel, copper (wiring), aluminium (structures) |
| **GDP growth** | India fastest-growing large economy; metals demand is highly GDP-correlated | Rising per-capita metal consumption |
| **RBI rate trajectory** | Rate cutting cycle | Benefits real estate → steel demand; lower rates reduce borrowing cost for capex-heavy producers |
| **Global commodity prices** | Copper near highs; aluminium approaching $3,000/t; steel stable | Directly impacts revenue for LME-linked producers |
| **Currency (INR/USD)** | — | Weakening INR helps aluminium/zinc exporters; strengthening INR helps copper importers |

### Policy Drivers

| Policy | Date | Impact |
|--------|------|--------|
| **12% safeguard duty on steel imports** | April 2025 (200 days, now expired) | Reduced steel imports by 38.7% in H1 FY26; reinstatement at 11-12% under evaluation |
| **Anti-dumping duties** | Ongoing (2025-2026) | Chinese electrical steel ($223/t), Vietnamese hot-rolled steel (5 years), low-ash met coke from 6 countries |
| **MMDR Amendment Act 2025** | Effective Sept 1, 2025 | Allows adding critical minerals to existing leases; mineral trading via exchanges; deep-seated mineral extraction |
| **National Critical Mineral Mission** | Jan 2025, 7-year horizon | ₹16,300 crore outlay; 30 critical minerals identified; reduces import dependence |
| **300 MT steel capacity target** | By 2030 | From current 235 MT; requires massive investment in new capacity |
| **Minerals Concession Rules 2026** | March 30, 2026 | Contiguous area addition to mining leases; associated minerals inclusion |

### Demand Drivers (structural)

| Demand Source | Metal Affected | Timeframe |
|---------------|---------------|-----------|
| **Infrastructure (roads, railways, smart cities)** | Steel, copper | Ongoing — ₹11.2T capex |
| **Energy transition (solar, wind, EVs)** | Copper (wiring, motors), aluminium (EV bodies, solar frames) | Structural — copper demand may triple by 2045 |
| **Data centers / AI infrastructure** | Copper | 500,000+ tonnes/year by 2030 |
| **Construction boom (Tier 2-3 cities)** | Steel | Medium-term — housing + commercial |
| **Automobile production (incl. EVs)** | Steel, aluminium, copper | Structural shift from ICE to EV increases metal content per vehicle |
| **Make in India / PLI manufacturing** | All metals | Policy-driven domestic manufacturing expansion |

### Supply Drivers

| Factor | Current State | Implication |
|--------|--------------|-------------|
| **India steel capacity** | 235 MT installed; 75.6% utilization (Q1 2026) | Mid-cycle — room for demand growth before new capacity needed |
| **China aluminium production cap** | China has hit capacity ceiling | Global aluminium supply constrained; benefits Indian producers |
| **Copper structural deficit** | 600,000 tonne deficit in 2026; India imports 90-95% concentrate | Bullish for copper prices; Hindustan Copper's mine expansion is critical |
| **India capacity additions planned** | Steel: +65 MT by 2030; Aluminium: +2 MTPA over 5 years | Supply growth coming but lagging demand growth |

## 1.4 — Cyclical vs Defensive Classification

| Sub-Group | Classification | Why | Momentum Implication |
|-----------|---------------|-----|---------------------|
| **A — Steel** | Highly cyclical + interest-rate sensitive | Revenue swings with construction/infra cycle; margins depend on steel-ore spread | Biggest moves in both directions; best for momentum in upcycle |
| **B — Aluminium** | Highly cyclical + commodity-linked | LME-priced; power cost is major margin driver | High beta; supply-constrained = stronger floor than steel |
| **B — Copper** | Structural demand + cyclical pricing | Energy transition creates secular demand; but short-term pricing is cyclical | More structural bull case than pure cycle |
| **B — Zinc/Silver** | Commodity-linked (zinc cyclical; silver dual: industrial + monetary) | Zinc follows industrial demand; silver benefits from both industrial use and precious metal demand | Mixed — zinc is cyclical, silver adds defensive hedge |
| **C — Mining (Ore)** | Highly cyclical (derived demand) | Ore demand = f(steel/metal production) | Leveraged play on steel cycle — moves more than steel itself |

| **Company capex** | JSW $2.5B in FY27; Tata targeting 40 MTPA by 2030 | Companies investing in expansion = confidence in demand |

### Cycle Verdict

```
Steel:      [Recovery] ----[▶ CURRENT: Early-to-Mid Upcycle]---- [Late Cycle] ---- [Downturn]
Aluminium:  [Recovery] ----[▶ CURRENT: Mid Upcycle, Supply-Constrained]---- [Late] ---- [Down]
Copper:     [▶ CURRENT: Structural Bull] — deficit-driven, not purely cyclical
Zinc:       [Recovery] ----[▶ CURRENT: Early-Mid Upcycle]---- [Late] ---- [Down]

```

**Key insight:** We are NOT late in the cycle. Capacity utilization at 75.6% has room to reach 85%+ before overheating. Demand growth (8-9%) exceeds capacity additions (which won't come online until 2028-2030). This is the phase where earnings accelerate and momentum trades work best.

## 1.7 — Key Risks

| # | Risk | Severity | Sub-Groups Affected | Mitigation |
|---|------|----------|--------------------|-----------| 
| 1 | **Chinese steel dumping** — 50MT+ surplus seeking markets | HIGH | Steel (A), Mining (C) | Safeguard duties (expired; reinstatement under evaluation); anti-dumping duties in place |
| 2 | **Safeguard duty expiry** — 12% duty lapsed; import surge possible | HIGH | Steel (A) | Government evaluating 11-12% reinstatement; DGTR investigations ongoing |
| 3 | **Global commodity price crash** — LME prices can drop 20-30% in months | MEDIUM | All sub-groups | Domestic demand insulates partially; not fully hedged |
| 4 | **China economic slowdown** — reduced Chinese metal demand depresses global prices | MEDIUM | Non-ferrous (B), Mining (C) | India's domestic demand is the primary driver, not exports |
| 5 | **Green steel transition cost** — BF-BOF to EAF requires massive capex | MEDIUM-LONG | Steel (A) | Gradual transition; hydrogen steel still early-stage |
| 6 | **Environmental / regulatory risk** — mining bans, clearance delays, ESG compliance costs | MEDIUM | Mining (C), Non-ferrous (B) | MMDR reforms easing process; but state-level unpredictability remains |
| 7 | **Input cost spikes** — coking coal, power, alumina price surges compress margins | MEDIUM | Steel (A), Aluminium (B) | Partially offset by steel/aluminium price pass-through |
| 8 | **Currency appreciation** — INR strengthening hurts metal exporters | LOW-MEDIUM | Aluminium exporters (Hindalco/Novelis) | Diversified revenue base |
| 9 | **PSU governance risk** — government interference, suboptimal capital allocation | LOW-MEDIUM | NMDC, SAIL, MOIL, NALCO, HCL | Structural risk for PSUs; private players less affected |

## 1.8 — Implications for Screening & Deep Study

Based on Phase 1 findings, the following adjustments apply when we reach Stage 1/Stage 2 screening and the 6-module deep study:

### Screening Adjustments
1. **Score stocks against sub-group peers** — a steel company vs steel companies, not vs Hindustan Zinc
2. **Debt/Equity thresholds are sector-specific** — steel companies carry higher D/E (capital-intensive); don't penalize for D/E 0.8-1.2 which is normal for steel
3. **PSU stocks** (SAIL, NMDC, MOIL, NALCO, HCL) need Module 6 (Governance) scrutiny — government shareholding is both stability and governance risk

### Module Adjustments
| Module | Sub-Group A (Steel) | Sub-Group B (Non-Ferrous) | Sub-Group C (Mining) |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| M1 — Momentum | Compare vs steel peers + Nifty Metal | Compare vs non-ferrous peers | Compare vs mining peers |
| M3 — Technicals | Standard | Standard | May have lower volume/liquidity for small miners |
| M4 — Fundamentals | Watch: steel-ore spread, capacity utilization, D/E | Watch: LME realization, power cost per tonne, smelter utilization | Watch: ore grade, extraction cost, reserve life |
| M5 — Valuation | PE less meaningful for cyclicals; use EV/EBITDA and P/B | EV/EBITDA preferred; high PE at cycle bottom is normal | Simple — P/B and EV/tonne of reserves |
| M6 — Governance | Less critical for private (Tata, JSW) | Vedanta governance needs scrutiny (Anil Agarwal) | Critical for PSUs (NMDC, MOIL, HCL) |

### Sub-Group Priority for Momentum Trading
1. **Non-Ferrous (B)** — strongest structural case (supply constraints + energy transition demand) + oligopoly pricing power
2. **Steel (A)** — largest stock universe, highest liquidity, classic momentum plays in upcycle
3. **Mining (C)** — leveraged play on steel but limited stock universe and PSU governance risk
