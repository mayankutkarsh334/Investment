# Deep Study Plan — International Funds

## Context
8 funds shortlisted after 2-stage screening (53 → 10 → 8, all active). Same 6-module weighted framework as FlexiCap and SmallCap, but with material adjustments for the international / offshore-feeder context.

**Investment goal:** ₹20,000/month SIP, 10+ year horizon. The **third co-equal sleeve** — a geographic / currency diversifier alongside the FlexiCap core and the SmallCap satellite.

**The defining context (read first):** This is an **all-active study** because the SEBI $7bn overseas cap has closed every passive index fund to SIP (see [screening/methodology.md](screening/methodology.md)). Most survivors are **offshore feeder funds** — an Indian wrapper investing into an *underlying foreign mutual fund*. The real engine is the underlying; the framework must **look through** to it.

### Module 1 — Returns Consistency (25%)

**What's the same:** CAGR 3Y/5Y/10Y, 3Y rolling average, alpha vs benchmark, returns vs sub-category, calendar-year decomposition.

**What's different for International:**

| Dimension | Domestic | International |
|-----------|----------|--------------|
| Benchmark | Nifty 500 / SmallCap 250 | **The fund's own foreign index** (Russell, S&P 500, MSCI World/EM/Europe) — NEVER compare across geographies |
| Key return driver to isolate | Stock selection | **Currency: how much CAGR is the foreign market vs ₹ depreciation (~3–4%/yr vs USD)** |
| Key stress years | 2018 (SC) / 2022 (FC) | **2022 global bear** (Fed hikes, NASDAQ −33%); **2020 COVID**; for EM: **2021–24 China/EM drawdown** |
| Skill test | Alpha vs Nifty | **Alpha vs own index** — does the active feeder beat the index it could be replaced by? |
| Inception bias | Moderate | **High** — many launched in the 2020–21 global-tech boom; CAGR flattered |

**Currency decomposition (mandatory):** Split the reported INR CAGR into (a) underlying foreign-market return in local currency, (b) ₹/$ (or ₹/€) move. A fund can post a good INR number on a flat foreign market purely via rupee weakness — and a rupee *appreciation* phase would reverse that.

**Module 1 Scorecard:**

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| 5Y CAGR vs own benchmark (alpha) | High | Beats own index by >2% = 5; matches = 3; lags = 2 |
| 10Y CAGR (if available) | High | Genuine long record through 2020 + 2022 = higher |
| Currency-adjusted return quality | High | Return that holds up ex-INR-depreciation = 5 |
| 3Y rolling average | Medium | Geography-aware vs peers |
| Consistency (no horrible year vs own index) | Medium | Worst year vs benchmark |
| Inception-bias adjustment | Modifier | Discount 2020–21 launches that missed earlier bears |

### Module 2 — Risk Profile (20%)

**What's different for International:**

| Risk metric | Domestic "good" | International note |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Max Drawdown | < 35% (FC) / < 40% (SC) | Benchmark-dependent — US tech can draw 45%+; judge **vs own index** |
| Volatility | < 13–16% | Includes **currency volatility** — a second axis of risk |
| Beta | vs Nifty | **vs own foreign index** (a feeder's beta to Nifty is near-meaningless) |
| Tracking error | n/a (active domestic) | **Critical** — for feeders, high TE vs the index = active risk *and* feeder/cash-drag friction |
| Down-capture | vs Nifty | vs own index — does the active feeder protect better than the index? |

**International-specific risk questions:**
1. **Currency tail risk** — what happens in a ₹ *appreciation* regime? Quantify the FX contribution to drawdown vs recovery.
2. **Concentration of the underlying** — a FANG+/tech feeder is 10 stocks; an MSCI World feeder is 1,400. Very different single-name risk.
3. **Liquidity / redemption mechanics** — feeders settle on the underlying's NAV with a lag; gating risk during a foreign-market crisis.
4. **Geographic/political risk** — EM (China policy, sanctions), single-country (Brazil, Taiwan) carry idiosyncratic tail risk a broad fund doesn't.

**Module 2 Scorecard:**

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Max Drawdown vs own index | High | Protects vs index = 5; worse than index = 2 |
| Sharpe / Sortino | High | ≥1.0 Sharpe = strong (intl base is higher) |
| Down-capture vs own benchmark | Critical | < 90% = 5 |
| Currency-risk assessment | Medium | Lower FX dependence + ₹-appreciation resilience = higher |
| Volatility (incl. FX) | Medium | Below own-category = higher |
| Concentration / look-through single-name risk | Medium | Diversified underlying = higher |

### Module 4 — Cost & AUM / Structure (20%)

**The most-adapted module.** International cost is structurally higher and double-layered; AUM works differently.

**True all-in cost = Indian feeder ER + embedded underlying-fund TER.** A 1.48% feeder over a 0.85% underlying ≈ **~2.3% true all-in** — vs a (closed) passive NASDAQ feeder at 0.19%. This gap is the central cost story of the international sleeve.

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Indian feeder ER (Direct) | High | ≤0.5% = 5; 0.5–1.0% = 4; 1.0–1.4% = 3; >1.4% = 2 |
| **Embedded underlying TER** | High | Look through to the underlying fund's fee; add to feeder ER |
| **True all-in cost** | Critical | < 1.0% = 5; 1.0–1.5% = 4; 1.5–2.0% = 3; > 2.0% = 2 |
| **Taxation treatment** | High | Post-2023/25/26 FoF tax rules; equity-orientation of underlying; LTCG vs slab |
| AUM (viability, not capacity) | Medium | ≥ ₹100 Cr viable; no upper penalty (liquid foreign markets) |
| **SIP-reopening / closure stability** | Medium | Has the fund closed/reopened with the SEBI cap, disrupting SIPs? Stable-open = 5 |
| Exit load | Low | Standard 1%/365d |

> **No AUM-capacity penalty** (unlike SmallCap) — foreign markets are liquid. But **closure stability is a new cost-adjacent risk:** a fund that pauses SIPs when the cap fills is a real disruption to a 10-year plan.

### Module 3 — Portfolio DNA & Look-Through (15%)

**The feeder look-through is the whole module.** What you actually own is the *underlying* fund's portfolio.

| Sub-dimension | Weight | What to examine |
|---------------|--------|-----------------|
| Underlying fund identity & mandate | High | Which foreign fund does the feeder buy? (JPMorgan, BlackRock, Franklin in-house, Edelweiss-affiliated, ETF) |
| Geographic / sector composition | High | Actual country & sector weights of the underlying |
| Concentration | Medium | Top-10 of the underlying; single-name risk |
| Style (growth/value/quality/tech) | Medium | Does it diversify the existing domestic sleeves? |
| **Overlap with domestic sleeves** | High | A US-tech or India-heavy fund may duplicate, not diversify |
| Cash drag at feeder level | Low | Feeder cash held outside the underlying |
| Number of holdings | Low | 10 (FANG+) vs 1,400 (MSCI World) — risk profile |

> **Diversification is the point.** Score *higher* for genuine non-correlation with the FlexiCap + SmallCap sleeves; score *lower* for funds (e.g. India+World Healthcare) that re-import Indian-equity risk.

### Module 5 — Manager Quality (feeder + underlying) (15%)

**Two layers to assess:**

1. **The underlying fund's manager** — the real stock-picker. Tenure, process, the foreign fund's own track record (often longer/cleaner than the Indian feeder's). This is where the alpha is (or isn't) made.
2. **The Indian feeder manager** — typically a **passive operational role** (manages currency, cash, subscriptions). Low individual alpha contribution, but matters for tracking efficiency and feeder discipline.

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Underlying fund's manager/process quality | High | Long-tenured, proven foreign manager = 5 |
| Underlying fund's own long-term record | High | The foreign fund's multi-cycle history |
| Feeder manager operational competence | Medium | Low tracking error, disciplined currency/cash mgmt |
| Continuity of the underlying mandate | Medium | Has the feeder switched underlying funds? (a red flag) |
| Skin-in-game / alignment | Low | At both layers |

---

### Module 6 — AMC Trustworthiness (5%)

Same framework as domestic, plus international-specific points:

1. **Offshore-partner quality** — the foreign AMC behind the underlying (its global standing, regulation).
2. **Feeder-suite breadth & commitment** — does the Indian AMC treat international as a serious long-term franchise or a fad launch? (Several AMCs launched theme funds at tops.)
3. **Closure/reopening conduct** — did the AMC manage the SEBI-cap closures transparently and fairly to SIP investors?
4. **Carry-forward findings:** Edelweiss, HSBC, ICICI, Franklin, PGIM AMCs are studied in FlexiCap/SmallCap — carry those AMC findings forward, update with international-specific data. **Note the heavy Edelweiss concentration (5 of 8 shortlisted)** — the AMC's governance flags from the domestic studies apply across most of this sleeve.
