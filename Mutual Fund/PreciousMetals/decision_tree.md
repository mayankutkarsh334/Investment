# Precious Metals (Gold & Silver) — Decision Tree

**Phase 3 of the sixth category study · 44 schemes screened · Phase 2 deliberately not run · written Aug 2026**

> **⚠ Not investment advice.** This document sets out mechanics and evidence, and names the forks. The choice is the reader's. I am not a licensed advisor.
>
> **This study produced no fund scores, and that is the finding.** Where the five prior studies ranked funds on manager skill, this category has none to rank — and the one dimension that could have separated schemes, tracking fidelity, turned out not to. **Nothing here is comparable to the equity or multi-asset scores.**

---

## The headline

**The sleeve is worth having. The scheme you fill it with is not worth agonising over.**

Adding a gold sleeve to the actual six-fund book cut portfolio volatility from 14.44% to 12.96% and max drawdown from −18.54% to −14.73% — **and that risk reduction held at every gold-return assumption tested, from 5%/yr to the realised 30%.** Silver did the opposite. And across sixteen gold vehicles measured over nineteen overlapping three-year windows, **the between-scheme spread (0.42pp) was smaller than the measurement uncertainty (0.43pp).**

This is the third time this repo has reached a null result on a category's defining skill claim — after MidCap's *"no fund has statistically significant alpha"* and MultiAsset's *"no allocation alpha anywhere."* Here: **no measurable tracking-fidelity edge anywhere.**

## Q1 — Does a gold sleeve earn a slot?

Settled by the MultiAsset study, then re-tested here against the **actual** book rather than a modelled core.

**Core = Edelweiss US Tech 6.7 / PP 26.7 / BOI Flexi 13.3 / Invesco Mid 13.3 / DSP Small 24 / BOI Small 16.** Candidate added at 13.3%, core diluted proportionally, annual rebalancing, 4.13y (the longest window silver permits).

| Sleeve at 13.3% | CAGR | Vol | Sharpe | ΔSharpe | MaxDD | ΔDD |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| **CORE (no sleeve)** | 18.93% | 14.44% | 0.86 | — | −18.54% | — |
| **Gold 100%** | 21.16% | **12.96%** | **1.13** | **+0.27** | **−14.73%** | **+3.82** |
| Gold 60 / Debt 40 | 19.74% | 12.70% | 1.04 | +0.18 | −15.17% | +3.37 |

**Worth more here than the MultiAsset study measured** — +0.27 against the real book versus +0.20/+0.11/+0.08 against its modelled core, because the real book holds 6.7% international rather than 20% and is therefore more equity-concentrated.

### ⭐ But the case is a risk case, not a Sharpe case

Both metals ran extraordinarily in the window. Haircutting them to a common return, preserving volatility and correlation structure:

| Metals haircut to | Gold ΔSharpe | **Gold ΔDD** |
|---|--:|--:|
| *realised (~30%)* | +0.27 | +3.82 |
| 12%/yr | +0.07 | +3.20 |
| 8%/yr | +0.03 | **+3.04** |
| 5%/yr | −0.01 | **+2.91** |

**The Sharpe gain is almost entirely regime — at ~8%/yr gold it vanishes. The volatility and drawdown reduction are regime-independent.**

> **Verdict on Q1: yes.** The honest statement of what it buys: **~1.5pp less portfolio volatility and ~3pp less drawdown, permanently.** Whether it also raises risk-adjusted return depends on a view about gold that nobody has. Anyone citing "+0.27 Sharpe" as the reason to own gold is citing 2022–26.

## Q2 — Gold only, or gold and silver?

| Sleeve at 13.3% | CAGR | Vol | ΔSharpe | MaxDD | ΔDD |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| Gold 100% | 21.16% | **12.96%** | +0.27 | **−14.73%** | **+3.82** |
| Gold 50 / Silver 50 | 21.98% | 13.86% | +0.26 | −15.58% | +2.97 |
| **Silver 100%** | **22.75%** | ⚠ **15.08%** | +0.22 | ⚠ **−19.74%** | ⚠ **−1.20** |

**Silver posted the best return of anything tested and still fails**, because it is the only candidate that makes the book *riskier*: volatility above the core's own, and a deeper drawdown. Standalone it runs **1.8× gold's volatility** (34.49% vs 19.09%), a **−45.53%** drawdown, and **twice gold's correlation to the core** (+0.16 vs +0.08) — consistent with roughly half of silver demand being industrial.

The gold > blends > silver ordering held at every return assumption, so this is not a window artefact. Silver also *failed during its own bull market*, which strengthens rather than weakens the result.

> **Verdict on Q2: gold only.** This slot exists to reduce risk. Silver is a return-seeking asset, and 40% of the book's flows already go to small cap.
>
> *Recorded, not recommended:* a 25% silver tilt inside the sleeve is roughly Sharpe-neutral at realised returns and degrades under haircut. It adds a second scheme to monitor for a marginal gain.

**⚠ Limitation:** one window, 4.13 years — silver FoFs did not exist before Feb 2022. MultiAsset used three windows; silver permits one.

## Q3 — ETF or FoF?

**A pure cost decision, and a small one.** Modelled at ₹10,000/month over 10 years with FIFO lot tracking:

| Component | Finding |
|---|---|
| **The 12- vs 24-month LTCG gate** | **₹525–₹1,959** over ten years with annual trimming — **₹0** under the flow-rebalancing policy in Q5. FIFO means every rebalance from the third onward sells units long-term under either gate |
| **The whole wrapper decision** | **₹3,000–₹28,000 over ten years on a ~₹15L corpus — 0.2% to 1.9%** |
| **Crossover** | The FoF wins if its own layer costs less than ~0.10–0.25%/yr, depending on ETF entry friction and demat charges |
| **Measured, not disclosed** | Best-FoF vs best-ETF realised gap ≈ **0.1pp** — smaller than the spread *within* either wrapper |

**⚠ Stated expense ratios do not predict realised drag.** Nippon's FoF layer is genuinely the cheapest disclosed (0.05%, measured 0.01%) — but Gold BeES, the ETF beneath it, is the second-worst tracker of eight. Screening this category on published ERs picks the wrong fund.

> **Verdict on Q3: for a monthly SIP, the FoF, on mechanics rather than cost.** It is SIP-native, needs no demat, buys at NAV, and pays no bid-ask spread or premium on 120 purchases. The ETF's advantages — a 12-month gate and a single fee layer — are worth ₹0 and ~0.1pp respectively under this book's policy.
>
> **The ETF is a defensible choice on identical evidence.** If a demat account already exists and monthly buying is not a chore, nothing here argues against it.

## Q4 — Which scheme?

### ⭐ The finding: they are not distinguishable

Sixteen vehicles, nineteen overlapping three-year windows, 2014-06 → 2026-08, split-adjusted:

| | Value |
|---|--:|
| Spread of median tracking difference across schemes | **0.42pp** |
| Typical within-scheme IQR | **0.43pp** |
| **Signal / noise** | ⚠ **1.0×** |

An earlier pass reported 2.5× and a clean ranking. **That was an artefact of my own split adjustment**, which divided by the raw NAV ratio on split days and baked that day's gold move into the factor — ICICI's was off by 8.20%, Axis's by 2.70%. Correcting it moved Axis Gold ETF from 1.29% to 0.51% and collapsed the ranking.

**Selected medians, for the record** *(rank is not meaningful within ~0.4pp)*:

| Scheme | Type | Median TD | IQR |
|---|:--:|--:|---|
| ICICI Pru Gold ETF | ETF | 0.49% | [0.44, 0.63] |
| Axis Gold ETF | ETF | 0.51% | [0.45, 1.37] |
| ABSL Gold ETF | ETF | 0.56% | [0.40, 0.65] |
| Kotak Gold ETF | ETF | 0.57% | [0.49, 0.62] |
| HDFC Gold ETF | ETF | 0.59% | [0.50, 0.77] |
| ICICI Pru Gold ETF FoF | FoF | 0.65% | [0.33, 0.81] |
| Quantum Gold ETF | ETF | 0.68% | [0.59, 0.71] |
| **Nippon Gold BeES** | ETF | **0.70%** | **[0.67, 0.76]** |
| Nippon India Gold Savings | FoF | 0.70% | [0.38, 0.87] |
| SBI Gold ETF | ETF | 0.72% | [0.63, 0.76] |

**⚠ The benchmark is partly circular.** Gross gold was estimated as `mean(anchor ETF CAGR + its stated ER)`, which forces each anchor's tracking difference toward its own stated ER. **ICICI's leading 0.49% against a stated 0.50% is near-definitional, not independent evidence.** A true IBJA or MCX series is required before any of these levels should be trusted.

> **Verdict on Q4: choose on scale, liquidity and a SID-verified cost stack — not on tracking difference, because it does not vary.** Any large, low-cost, physically-backed vehicle from an established AMC is equivalent on the evidence.

### The one robust negative

**Nippon India Gold Savings and Gold BeES.** The FoF layer is genuinely cheap; the underlying ETF is not. Bottom of the table with the tightest measurement in it, and the 0.82% underlying-ER figure has now survived **four independent tests**. The widely-quoted 0.25% is wrong.

### Excluded on data integrity

**SBI Gold Fund** reports a −0.42% layer against its own ETF across all nineteen windows — not split-related, and its ETF is internally coherent (TD 0.72% vs a stated 0.70% ER). A FoF sitting on that cannot track at 0.30%. Unexplained; excluded. **Its apparent top ranking should be disregarded.**

## Q5 — Sizing, funding and mechanics

### Size

| Option | Gold as % of flows | Note |
|---|--:|---|
| ₹10,000 gold | 13.3% | Above MultiAsset's recommended 8–12% band |
| **₹7,500 gold + ₹2,500 short-duration debt** | **10.0%** | In band |

Debt contributes ~+0.01 Sharpe on its own and is taxed at slab. At realised gold returns the pure-gold sleeve is clearly better (+0.27 vs +0.18); **under an 8%/yr haircut the two converge (+0.03 each), and at 5%/yr the debt version wins.** The debt leg is a sizing and stability instrument, not a return one — and is redundant if EPF/PPF already runs.

### Funding

From SmallCap, per `Personal Finance/mutual_fund_sip_decisions_2026-07.md` §4b:

| Fund | From | To |
|---|--:|--:|
| DSP Small Cap | 18,000 | **12,000** |
| BOI Small Cap | 12,000 | **8,000** |
| **Gold sleeve** | — | **10,000** |

Preserves SmallCap's 60/40 anchor-satellite ratio. Look-through effect: **mid+small ~57% → ~44%**, small alone **~40% → ~29%**, non-equity **~4% → ~17%**, and **BOI at AMC level ₹22,000 → ₹18,000 (29.3% → 24%)**.

### Rebalancing — flow, never sell

**Direct the monthly SIP to whatever is underweight. Do not sell gold to rebalance.**

| Gold return | Peak gold weight under flow-only, target 10% |
|---|---|
| 8%/yr | 10.0% at 5y, 10y, 15y, 20y |
| 12%/yr | 10.0% throughout |
| 18%/yr | 10.0% at 10y, 12.2% at 20y |
| 25%/yr | 15.0% at 15y, 21.7% at 20y |

**Zero measurable drift up to an 18%/yr gold regime sustained for a decade**, because correcting a 10% sleeve requires redirecting amounts that stay small against annual contributions. Modelled on a **flat ₹75,000/month with no step-up**, which is the stated policy — any increase makes flows more powerful.

The mechanic is worth **±0.26%** of terminal wealth, so choose it for risk control and simplicity, not tax. **Backstop:** trim only if gold exceeds ~15% of the book. **Check quarterly, act at most annually** — an annual-only check missed a real 16.6% peak in back-testing.

### Excluded routes

**Sovereign Gold Bonds — out.** New issuance discontinued since Feb 2024. **Budget 2026 removed the capital-gains exemption for secondary-market buyers effective 1 April 2026**, so redemption at maturity is taxed at 12.5% exactly like an ETF. The coupon is paid on *nominal* value, so at current gold prices it is worth **0.42–0.69% net of slab, not 2.5%**. Maximum residual tenor is 5.6 years against a 10+ year sleeve, and no SIP is possible.

*For a lump sum matched to a tranche maturity, the discount-plus-coupon edge is real at roughly +0.3% to +1.3%/yr. That is a different decision.*

**Digital gold, physical, futures, mining equity — out.** Unregulated, cost-laden, leveraged, or equity-contaminated respectively.

## Review triggers

1. **Gold weight above ~15%** of the book — the only condition under which the sleeve is ever sold.
2. **⚠ Inflow restrictions tightening toward retail.** Six AMCs capped gold subscriptions from June 2026 (HDFC, ICICI, Nippon, Tata, Axis, ABSL) as category AUM grew ~195% YoY to ₹1.85 lakh crore and import duty rose 6% → 15%. **These do not bind at ₹7,500/month** — they target ₹25 Cr institutional subscriptions and ₹10 lakh/month FoF lump sums. **If they reach retail SIP levels, the sleeve's access assumption breaks.** This is the gold analogue of the International sleeve's SEBI cap.
3. **Expense-ratio drift on either layer** — verify against the SID, never an aggregator.
4. **Tracking difference deterioration** beyond the ~0.4pp noise band, sustained across windows.
5. **Two consecutive years without rebalancing** — inherited from MultiAsset; much easier to satisfy under flow-rebalancing, which involves no sell decision.
6. **Tax-regime change** on gold FoFs or ETFs.

## What this study established that the framework did not anticipate

1. **⭐ No measurable tracking-fidelity edge across sixteen vehicles.** Signal/noise 1.0×. The module carrying 35% of the study's weight measures something that does not vary between schemes.
2. **⭐ Stated expense ratios do not predict realised drag.** The cheapest disclosed FoF layer sat near the bottom on realised tracking, because the layer nobody quotes — the underlying ETF's — dominates.
3. **The wrapper question is near-moot.** The intra-wrapper spread exceeds the inter-wrapper difference.
4. **The tax gate is defused by FIFO**, not by wrapper choice — and entirely by never selling.
5. **Gold's benefit is regime-independent on risk and regime-dependent on Sharpe.** These are routinely conflated.
6. **⚠ My own data handling produced a false ranking twice** — once via daily tracking error that structurally penalised FoFs, once via a split estimator that baked market moves into split factors. **Both were caught only by testing the result rather than reporting it.**

---

## One-line verdict

**The gold sleeve earns its place on risk grounds that hold in every regime tested — roughly 1.5pp less portfolio volatility and 3pp less drawdown — silver does not and makes the book riskier, and the scheme filling the slot is close to irrelevant: across sixteen vehicles and nineteen windows the between-scheme spread was smaller than the measurement error, so the honest instruction is to pick any large, cheap, physically-backed vehicle, avoid the one with the expensive underlying ETF, fund it by trimming SmallCap, and never sell it to rebalance.**
