# Multi Asset Allocation — Decision Tree

**Phase 3 of the fifth category study · seven funds studied in full · written Aug 2026**

> **⚠ Not investment advice.** This document sets out mechanics and evidence, and names the forks. The choice is the reader's.
>
> **Scores in this study are NOT comparable to the four equity categories** — they use a multi-asset re-weight (Risk 25 · Returns 20 · Allocation 20 · Cost+Tax 20 · Manager 10 · AMC 5), measured against blended benchmarks and a DIY basket rather than a single equity index.

---

## The headline

**The evidence supports adding non-equity exposure to the portfolio. It does not support buying a multi-asset fund to get it.**

Across three independent windows, a plain do-it-yourself basket of a gold ETF plus a short-duration debt fund improved the whole portfolio's risk-adjusted return **more than any of the seven studied funds** in two windows, and was second only to Quant in the third. **Several funds improved it by nothing at all, and two made it worse.**

This is the outcome the study plan explicitly anticipated as legitimate: *"If none did, the recommendation is DIY — a legitimate, category-defining outcome."*

---

## Q1 — Does a multi-asset sleeve earn a slot in the ₹65K/month budget?

**The test that matters is not how a fund performs standalone — it is what it does to a portfolio that already holds four equity sleeves.** Every fund's Module 1 and Module 2 measured the former. This is the latter.

**Core portfolio modelled as:** 30% Parag Parikh FlexiCap · 25% Edelweiss MidCap · 25% DSP SmallCap · 20% international, annually rebalanced. Each candidate is then added at **20% of the total**, diluting the core proportionally.

### Whole-portfolio Sharpe improvement over core, by window

| Sleeve added (20%) | **A: 3.19y** *(all 7 funds)* | **B: 5.91y** *(incl. 2022)* | **C: 6.89y** *(incl. COVID)* |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DIY — 12% gold + 8% short debt** | **+0.20** 🥇 | **+0.11** 🥇 | +0.08 |
| **DIY — 10% gold + 10% short debt** | **+0.18** | +0.10 | +0.07 |
| **DIY — 6% gold + 14% short debt** | — | +0.08 | +0.05 |
| Quant Multi Asset | +0.10 | +0.10 | **+0.11** 🥇 |
| WOC Multi Asset | +0.10 | *n/a* | *n/a* |
| ICICI Pru Multi-Asset | +0.03 | +0.08 | +0.05 |
| SBI Multi Asset | +0.05 | +0.03 | −0.00 |
| Nippon Multi Asset | +0.04 | +0.02 | *n/a* |
| *+20% pure short-duration debt* | *+0.01* | *+0.01* | *−0.01* |
| UTI Multi Asset | +0.00 | ⚠ **−0.02** | ⚠ **−0.04** |
| ABSL Multi Asset | ⚠ **−0.01** | *n/a* | *n/a* |

**Three findings, in order of importance:**

**1. The non-equity exposure is worth having.** Every DIY variant improved whole-portfolio Sharpe in every window (+0.05 to +0.20), cut volatility by 1.3–2.1 percentage points, and reduced maximum drawdown by 4.2–4.6 points. The portfolio is ~100% equity and it shows.

**2. ⚠ Gold does essentially all the work; debt alone does almost nothing.** A 20% pure short-duration-debt sleeve changed whole-portfolio Sharpe by **+0.01, +0.01 and −0.01** across the three windows. It lowers volatility and lowers return by almost exactly as much. **The diversification benefit in this category comes from gold's decorrelation, not from debt.**

**3. ⚠ The funds underperform the DIY basket because they duplicate what you already own.** They hold **26% to 68% equity** — and M2 measured correlations to Parag Parikh FlexiCap of 0.77 to 0.93. A DIY gold-and-debt sleeve is 100% non-equity. **You are paying a fund to give you back the asset class you were trying to dilute.**

> **Verdict on Q1: yes, a non-equity sleeve earns a slot — approximately 15–20% of the portfolio. No, a multi-asset *fund* is not the efficient way to fill it.**

## Q2 — Fund vs DIY: did any fund beat the naïve basket post-tax?

### On standalone post-tax returns — most did, but the margin shrinks with the window

| Fund | Post-tax DIY edge, **5.91y** (incl. 2022) | Post-tax DIY edge, **3.19y** |
|---|---|---|
| Quant | **+₹14,88,126** | +₹3,69,517 |
| ICICI Pru | +₹7,11,730 | +₹1,09,142 |
| Nippon | +₹3,21,833 | +₹2,22,287 |
| UTI | ⚠ **−₹1,061** | +₹1,24,119 |
| SBI | ⚠ **−₹18,914** | +₹91,068 |
| ABSL | *n/a* | +₹1,06,573 |
| WOC | *n/a* | +₹66,184 |

**Over the short window all seven beat DIY. Over the window containing a real bear year, only three of five did — SBI and UTI both lost.** The DIY counterfactual is materially harder than the young-fund comparison suggested.

### But standalone return is the wrong test, and this is the study's central finding

**Three structural claims justify the category. All three were measured, and all three are small or absent.**

| Claim | Measured value | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity taxation shelters the whole corpus** | **₹15,625 flat** per ₹10L against the middle tier five of seven funds occupy — the LTCG rate is 12.5% either way | ⚠ **Negligible.** Decisive only against *slab*, which no studied fund faces |
| **Internal rebalancing is tax-free** | **0.08–0.17pp/yr**, modelled with full cost-basis tracking | ⚠ **Minor** — but see the correction below: ~~the DIY investor's ₹1.25L annual exemption absorbs most of a modest rebalance~~ **⚠ CORRECTED Aug 2026: §112A's ₹1.25L exemption is a listed-equity benefit and does NOT apply to gold or silver at all.** The metal leg gets no shelter, so this drag is understated. *(PreciousMetals study)* |
| **Professional asset allocation adds value** | ⚠ **Zero, across seven of seven funds** | ❌ **Absent** — see below |

**⭐ The allocation-alpha finding, which is the most robust conclusion this study produced:**

| Fund | Allocation contribution | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| WOC | **−2.56%/yr** | Frozen dial (7.4pp range in a 10–80% mandate); risk-reducing |
| ICICI Pru | **−0.12%/yr** | Swinging dial (54.3pp range); risk-*additive* — drawdown 7pp deeper than its own average weights |
| UTI | **negative** | Documented model, refuted by outcomes |
| SBI, Nippon, ABSL, Quant | none demonstrable | — |

**And it replicates outside the category, which is what makes it hard to dismiss:**

| Balanced Advantage fund — the purest tactical-allocation product | vs a plain Nifty 500 index |
|---|---|
| ICICI Prudential Balanced Advantage (6.9y) | ⚠ **−2.53pp/yr** |
| WhiteOak Capital Balanced Advantage (3.5y) | ⚠ **−0.91pp/yr** |
| HDFC Balanced Advantage (6.9y) | Sharpe **0.72** vs pure equity's **1.01** |

**Three AMCs, three managers, three different models, one result.** At both WhiteOak and ICICI the *same team's* stock-selection funds beat their benchmarks by +2.5 to +7.9pp/yr while their allocation products failed.

> **Verdict on Q2: the funds that beat DIY did so on security selection, not on structure or allocation.** Since the structural advantages are worth roughly 0.1–0.2pp/yr combined, **a multi-asset fund is worth paying for only if you believe its manager can pick securities — and if that is the belief, an equity fund expresses it more directly and cheaply.**

## Q3 — Sub-type: conservative or equity-oriented?

**Moot under the DIY recommendation, but decisive if a fund is chosen anyway — and the answer is counter-intuitive.**

| Sub-type | Fund | Net equity | Whole-portfolio Sharpe gain | Correlation to FlexiCap core |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** | **WOC** | **26%** | **+0.10** | **0.77** *(lowest)* |
| Balanced | Quant | 53% | +0.10 | 0.79 |
| Balanced | SBI | 47% | +0.05 | 0.84 |
| Balanced | Nippon | 56% | +0.04 | 0.87 |
| Equity-oriented | ICICI Pru | 64% | +0.03 | 0.79 |
| Equity-oriented | ABSL | 68% | −0.01 | 0.84 |
| Equity-oriented | UTI | 66% | ⚠ **−0.02** | ⚠ **0.93** |

**The pattern is clean and it follows directly from Q1: the less equity a fund holds, the more it helps a portfolio that is already all equity.** The two equity-oriented funds contribute nothing or subtract. **UTI — 66% equity, 0.93 correlation to the core — is functionally a second FlexiCap holding with a fee.**

**And the tax logic runs backwards.** The equity-oriented sub-type earns equity taxation, which the study plan treated as the category's trump card. **Measured, that shelter is worth ₹15,625 per ₹10 lakh** — while the extra equity it requires costs 0.03 to 0.05 Sharpe points at the portfolio level. **The tax tail does not wag this dog.**

> **Verdict on Q3: if a fund is used, the conservative sub-type is correct — and the reason is duplication, not tax.**

**Two defensible fund choices, for two different jobs. Neither beats DIY on the portfolio test.**

**Not recommended despite ranking well:**
- **Quant (3.20)** posts the best whole-portfolio contribution of any fund — but its founder-CIO **settled a SEBI front-running case** (verified: accepted Aug-2025, ₹1–2 Cr each against ₹70–80 Cr of alleged transactions, no findings of fact established), and **37% of its "13.6-year record" belongs to Escorts Mutual Fund** and is unusable. AMC score 1.8.
- **SBI (3.66), Nippon (3.51), ABSL (3.18), UTI (3.03)** add ≤0.05 Sharpe, nothing, or negative.

### Sizing, if a fund is used

Within a **₹15–20K/month** sleeve out of the ₹65K budget: **the additive content is the non-equity portion, not the headline amount.** ₹18K/month into ICICI (64% equity) delivers roughly **₹6.5K of genuine non-equity**; into WOC (26% equity), roughly **₹13K**. Size to the non-equity contribution, not to AUM.

## Q5 — The DIY route, specified

**If the evidence is followed, this is what replaces the fund.**

| Leg | Weight of total portfolio | Vehicle | Approx. cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gold** | **8–12%** | A gold ETF (or gold index fund) | ~0.4–0.5% |
| **Short-duration debt** | **6–10%** | A short-duration or money-market debt fund | ~0.3% |
| **Total sleeve** | **~15–20%** | | **~0.4% blended** |

**Mechanics and honest caveats:**

1. **⚠ Do not size gold off this study's window.** Gold returned ~29%/yr over the measurement period, +71.85% in 2025 alone, then **−24.71% in Q1-2026**. Much of the DIY basket's measured advantage is that run, and it is not repeatable. **The conclusion survives at a 6% gold weight (+0.05 to +0.08 Sharpe), so the *direction* is robust — but the magnitude is regime-flattered.** Choose gold weight deliberately; do not maximise it because the backtest says so.
2. **Rebalance annually, not more.** The modelled tax drag on annual DIY rebalancing is **1.39–1.61pp/yr** in the realised tests and materially less in the forward model, ~~largely absorbed by the ₹1.25L LTCG exemption~~. **⚠ CORRECTED Aug 2026: the ₹1.25L exemption does not apply to metals** (§112A covers listed equity only), so the metal leg's drag is understated here. **The follow-on PreciousMetals study resolved this differently: do not sell the metal leg at all — rebalance by directing new SIP flows instead**, which defers the gain indefinitely and costs nothing in risk control up to an 18%/yr gold regime.
3. **Tax:** ~~gold ETFs get 12.5% LTCG after 12 months~~ **⚠ CORRECTED Aug 2026: 12 months applies to the listed ETF; a gold FoF's unlisted units carry a 24-month gate** (12.5% above it, slab below). The debt leg is taxed at slab. **This is the one place the fund route genuinely wins** — worth the 0.08–0.17pp/yr shield plus, for an equity-taxed fund, ₹15,625 per ₹10L. Real, and small. *(Under flow-rebalancing the gate is worth ₹0 — see [PreciousMetals](../PreciousMetals/decision_tree.md).)*
4. **The DIY route requires discipline the fund provides automatically.** If annual rebalancing will not actually happen, the fund's internal shield and one-decision simplicity are worth more than this analysis credits.

## Review triggers

**If the DIY route is taken:**
1. **Gold weight drift** — a 10% target becomes 15% after a run. Rebalance annually and mechanically.
2. **The debt leg's purpose** — it contributes ~0.01 Sharpe. It is there for stability and dry powder, not return. If rates make it genuinely attractive, size up deliberately.
3. **Whether annual rebalancing actually happens.** Two consecutive missed years and the fund route becomes the better choice.

**If a fund is held:**
4. **WOC** — the unresolved **0.39–0.67% expense ratio** (it decides the 10-year DIY test); the **24-month tax gate**; the **ETCD 30% ceiling** against 27.31% metals notional; and its first real bear market.
5. **ICICI** — **Sankaran Naren's tenure** (AMC-wide CIO, no disclosed succession, and the alpha is his); the **3.32pp gross-equity buffer** that constrains how defensive the fund may become; and whether the six-year de-risking continues.
6. **Any fund** — allocation drift toward closet equity (UTI is the cautionary case at 0.93 correlation), tax-status change, and debt-book credit deterioration.

**Standing, for both routes:**
7. **This category's central skill claim is unsupported.** If a fund ever demonstrates genuine allocation alpha over a full cycle, that is new information and this verdict should be revisited.

## What this study established that the framework did not anticipate

1. **⭐ No allocation alpha anywhere** — seven of seven funds, three failure modes, replicated across three Balanced Advantage funds at three AMCs. The category's defining skill claim is unsupported by every fund this study could test.
2. **The tax triad is worth ~0.1–0.2pp/yr, not the decisive edge the plan assumed.** Equity status separates the shortlist by ₹15,625 per ₹10L.
3. **Gold, not debt, does the diversification work.** A pure debt sleeve adds ~0.01 Sharpe.
4. **The DIY counterfactual is harder over longer windows** — over 5.91 years including 2022, two of five cycle-tested funds *lost* to it.
5. **⚠ The screen made one error.** ICICI Pru was eliminated on a 3-year window (adopted because most survivors lacked 5Y history) using a **Tickertape Sharpe of 0.29** contradicted by the AMC's own factsheet (1.10) and by MFAPI (1.51 over 5.91y). It is now the highest-scoring fund in the study. `screening/methodology.md` and `stage2_performance.md` require correction.
6. **Aggregator data failed repeatedly** — five stale expense ratios, two wrong Sharpe ratios, three wrong benchmarks, one wrong exit load. **Every conclusion that mattered had to be re-verified against SIDs and factsheets.**

---

## One-line verdict

**The portfolio needs the non-equity exposure and does not need a multi-asset fund to get it: a plain gold-ETF-plus-short-debt basket improved whole-portfolio risk-adjusted return more than any of the seven studied funds in two of three windows and was beaten only by Quant in the third — because the funds hold 26–68% equity that duplicates what the portfolio already owns, because the tax and rebalancing shields the category is sold on are worth roughly a tenth of a percent a year combined, and because not one of seven funds, nor any of three Balanced Advantage funds at three separate AMCs, could demonstrate that professional asset allocation adds value at all.**
