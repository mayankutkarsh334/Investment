# Screening Methodology: Variables That Matter

## Context
We are selecting a fund for a **₹20,000/month SIP** with a 10+ year horizon.
This changes what variables matter compared to a lumpsum investor.

---

## Key Variables & Why They Matter

### 1. Returns (But Context Matters)

| Metric | Why it matters for SIP |
|--------|------------------------|
| **CAGR 3Y / 5Y / 10Y** | Longer horizons are more reliable; 1Y is noise |
| **Rolling 3Y Avg Return** | Average across all 3-year windows — reduces recency bias. Superior to point-to-point CAGR |
| **Absolute 1Y / 6M / 3M** | Short-term momentum — useful but noisy |
| **Alpha** | Manager skill beyond benchmark. Negative = underperforming benchmark |
| **Returns vs Sub-category** | Consistency of outperformance vs peers across multiple periods |

> For SIP: Rolling returns matter more than point-to-point CAGR.

---

### 2. Risk Metrics

| Metric | Why it matters for SIP |
|--------|------------------------|
| **Volatility** | High volatility = more NAV swings = harder to stay invested |
| **Max Drawdown** | Worst peak-to-trough fall. SIP works only if you don't panic-exit during crashes |
| **Sharpe Ratio** | Return per unit of total risk. Higher = better quality returns |
| **Sortino Ratio** | Like Sharpe but only penalizes downside risk — more relevant for long-term investors |
| **Tracking Error** | Deviation from benchmark. Higher = more active bets |

> Max Drawdown is especially critical for SIP investors — a 40% fall tests whether you continue.

---

### 3. Cost

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|
| **Expense Ratio (ER)** | Deducted annually from NAV. Even 1% difference = ₹3–5 lakh over a 10-year ₹20K SIP |

> Cost is the only guaranteed drag on returns. Lower ER always wins, all else equal.

---

### 4. Portfolio Composition

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|
| **% Largecap / Midcap / Smallcap** | Defines risk-return profile. More mid+small = higher growth potential, more volatility |
| **Top 10 Holdings %** | Concentration risk. >70% = few big bets. <50% = diversified |
| **PE Ratio vs Category PE** | Cheap (below category) = value tilt; expensive = growth/momentum tilt |
| **% Cash Holding** | High cash = manager is cautious / waiting for opportunities |

---

### 5. Fund Quality Signals

| Metric | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|
| **Alpha** | Manager generating excess returns over benchmark |
| **AUM Size** | Very large = limits agility in mid/smallcap. Very small = sustainability risk |
| **Fund Age** | Young funds lack track record across multiple market cycles |
| **Fund Manager Tenure** | If the manager who built the track record left, history may not repeat |

---

## Decision Framework for ₹20K SIP

Priority order:
1. **Consistency > peak returns** — rolling returns, not point-to-point CAGR
2. **Drawdown tolerance** — fund whose worst fall you can psychologically survive
3. **Low expense ratio** — compounding works against you on costs
4. **Sharpe/Sortino ratio** — good returns per unit of risk taken
5. **Portfolio style match** — decide if you want largecap-heavy (stable) or mid/small-heavy (aggressive)
6. **AUM sweet spot** — ₹5,000–80,000 Cr generally healthy; avoid extremes
