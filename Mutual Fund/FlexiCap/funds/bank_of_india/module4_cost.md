Understanding why BOI's tiny AUM is an advantage, not a weakness, is the core insight of this module.

## The ER Anomaly — Why 0.52% at ₹2,388 Cr Is Unusually Cheap

Most small AMCs charge 0.80–1.20% for small equity funds — the fixed costs of compliance, auditing, custodian fees, and fund management are spread across a small AUM, necessitating higher ERs. BOI at 0.52% with ₹2,388 Cr is an anomaly. Three explanations:

```mermaid
pie title "Why Can BOI Charge 0.52% at ₹2,388 Cr? — Three Theories"
    "Parent Bank Cost Subsidy (Likely)" : 50
    "Lean Operations — Small Team" : 30
    "Loss-Leader Strategy — Attract Assets" : 20
```

**Theory 1 — Parent bank cost subsidy (most likely):**

Bank of India (the PSU bank parent) likely absorbs significant infrastructure costs — office space, IT systems, compliance staff, legal — through the broader banking entity. BOI AMC piggybacks on the bank's infrastructure at below-market cost, enabling a lower ER without commercial sacrifice. PSU bank subsidiaries in asset management frequently benefit from this structural subsidy.

**Theory 2 — Genuinely lean operations:**

Alok Singh manages 10 schemes with a small research team. If the team runs on 4–6 analysts covering 67 stocks (vs HDFC's 50+ analysts covering 500+ stocks), operating costs are proportionally lower. The trade-off is research depth — a risk flagged in the AMC section below.

**Theory 3 — Loss-leader strategy:**

BOI may be deliberately pricing below cost to attract assets and screen well on cost-filter searches. If successful, AUM grows → economics improve → ER rises to 0.68–0.80% once commercial sustainability is established. Investors should monitor ER annually.

**The monitoring imperative:** SEBI permits BOI to charge up to ~0.80% at its current AUM (35% above actual charge). If the ER creeps upward in 2027–2028 as AUM grows and commercial pressure increases, investors should reassess the cost advantage.

## SEBI TER Structure — BOI Charges 35% Below Regulatory Cap

SEBI's tiered TER structure sets maximum permitted expense ratios for equity funds by AUM slab. At ₹2,388 Cr:

| AUM Slab | Max TER Permitted (Equity, Direct) | BOI Covers |
|---------|-----------------------------------|------------|
| First ₹500 Cr | 1.00% | Yes |
| Next ₹250 Cr | 0.875% | Yes |
| Next ₹1,250 Cr | 0.75% | Yes |
| Remaining ₹388 Cr | 0.65% | Yes |
| **Blended SEBI Max (~₹2,388 Cr)** | **~0.80%** | — |
| **BOI Actual ER** | **0.52%** | — |
| **Discount vs SEBI Max** | **35% below cap** | Genuine cost discipline |

BOI is charging 35% below what SEBI legally permits at its current AUM. This is a strong positive signal — the fund is not raising ER to the regulatory maximum (as many smaller AMCs do), but instead passing cost savings to investors.

**As AUM grows, the SEBI cap drops** — but BOI is already well below future thresholds:
- At ₹5,000 Cr: SEBI max ~0.70%; BOI at 0.52% = still 26% below cap
- At ₹10,000 Cr: SEBI max ~0.65%; BOI at 0.52% = still 20% below cap

Unless the AMC explicitly raises the ER, the cost advantage relative to regulatory limits persists as AUM grows.
