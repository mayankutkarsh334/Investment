### Module 2 — Risk Profile (20%)

**What's different for Small Cap:**

**Benchmark for "acceptable" max drawdown shifts dramatically:**

| Risk Metric | FlexiCap "Good" | Small Cap "Good" | Small Cap "Alarming" |
|-------------|-----------------|------------------|---------------------|
| Max Drawdown | < 35% | < 40% | > 55% |
| Volatility | < 13% | < 16% | > 19% |
| Category St Dev | ~14% | ~16% | — |
| Beta | < 0.9 = low | < 1.1 = low | > 1.3 = high |

**For Small Cap, the key questions are:**
1. **Recovery time from max drawdown** — small cap funds can take 2-3 years to recover from a crash (vs 5 weeks for Parag Parikh FlexiCap in COVID). What is the fund's historical recovery duration?
2. **Downside capture ratio** — the single most important metric. PP FlexiCap had 59% downside capture. For small cap, < 85% is excellent, 85-100% is average, > 100% is poor. Are there any small cap funds with asymmetric capture like PP?
3. **2018-2019 drawdown specifically** — only funds with pre-2019 inception have this data. For the 4 funds with no 2018 data, the risk profile is incomplete.
4. **Redemption risk** — small cap funds with low AUM (BOI at ₹1,770 Cr, Union at ₹1,980 Cr) face higher redemption pressure during crashes. A 20% redemption in 2 weeks forces selling small-cap positions at distressed prices, worsening NAV further. Document exit load and cash holding as redemption buffers.

**Module 2 Scorecard — Sub-dimensions:**

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Max Drawdown (adjusted for inception) | High | <35% = 5, 35-45% = 4, 45-55% = 3, >55% = 2 |
| Volatility vs category | Medium | Below category avg = 5, at avg = 3, above = 2 |
| Sharpe Ratio | High | ≥0.7 = 5, 0.5-0.7 = 4, 0.3-0.5 = 3, 0.1-0.3 = 2, <0.1 = 1 |
| Sortino Ratio | Medium | Same scale |
| Downside Capture Ratio | Critical | < 85% = 5, 85-95% = 4, 95-105% = 3, > 105% = 2 |
| Recovery time from max DD | Medium | < 12 months = 5, 12-18 mo = 4, 18-24 mo = 3, > 24 mo = 2 |
| 2018 drawdown (if available) | Critical | Survived with < 50% fall and < 24 month recovery = 5 |
| Redemption risk (AUM × exit load) | Low | Larger exit load + adequate cash = lower risk |

### Module 3 — Portfolio DNA (15%)

**What's fundamentally different for Small Cap:**

SEBI mandates 65%+ in small caps — so the basic allocation question from FlexiCap ("how much mid/large cap?") is reversed. For small cap, the questions are:

1. **How deep into small cap?** SEBI defines small cap as the 251st company onwards. But the 251st company (rank by market cap) is very different from the 600th:
   - 250th-350th ranked companies: often ₹3,000-6,000 Cr market cap — safer, more liquid, but lower growth potential
   - 350th-600th ranked: ₹1,000-3,000 Cr — genuine small caps with high growth potential and higher risk
   - 600th+: ₹100-1,000 Cr — very illiquid, high concentration risk, maximum growth potential
   - Look for: number of stocks, top-10 concentration, smallest holding size

2. **What's the manager doing with the remaining 35%?**
   - Mid-cap allocation (tactical vs strategic)
   - Large-cap buffer (for liquidity during redemptions)
   - Cash holding (dry powder for opportunities vs forced idle cash)
   - Debt/bonds (defensive, unusual for small cap equity funds)

3. **Portfolio turnover** — small caps are illiquid. High turnover (> 80%) means the manager is buying and selling positions that are hard to move without price impact. This creates hidden transaction costs not captured in the ER.

4. **Top-10 concentration** — for small cap, 25-35% in top 10 is reasonable. Above 45% = concentrated, below 20% = overdiversified.

5. **Number of stocks** — 50-80 stocks is the sweet spot. Below 40 = concentration risk. Above 100 = forced overdiversification to deploy capital, often into lower-quality names.

**Module 3 Scorecard — Sub-dimensions:**

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Genuine small cap depth | High | Does the portfolio go beyond 350th rank? Analyse avg mkt cap of holdings |
| Smallcap % (how well does it honour the mandate) | Medium | 70%+ = 5, 65-70% = 4 (minimum), < 65% = regulatory flag |
| Mid-cap allocation (quality of the 35%) | Medium | Strategic mid-cap for quality = 5; padding with large-caps = 3 |
| Cash & liquidity management | High | Adequate cash (3-8%) for redemptions without forced selling = 5 |
| Portfolio turnover | Medium | < 40% = 5, 40-70% = 4, 70-100% = 3, > 100% = 2 |
| Top-10 concentration | Medium | 25-35% = 5, 35-45% = 4, > 45% or < 20% = 3 |
| Number of stocks | Low | 50-80 = 5, 80-100 = 4, > 100 or < 40 = 3 |
| Sector diversification | Medium | No single sector > 25% of portfolio |
| Portfolio PE vs category | Low | Below category PE = discipline (not always good in small cap) |

### Module 4 — Cost & AUM Impact (20%)

**AUM analysis is fundamentally different from FlexiCap:**

**The Small Cap AUM Sweet Spot:**

| AUM Range | Status | Reasoning |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| < ₹500 Cr | Too small | Operational risk, redemption spiral risk |
| ₹500 – 2,000 Cr | Small but manageable | Maximum agility; risk of AUM outflows in stress |
| ₹2,000 – 12,000 Cr | **Sweet spot** | Can buy/sell genuine small-caps; manageable SIP inflows |
| ₹12,000 – 20,000 Cr | Approaching constraint | Mid-range small-caps only; true 400th-600th rank harder |
| ₹20,000 – 30,000 Cr | Constrained | Gravitates toward 250th-350th (large end of small cap) |
| > ₹30,000 Cr | Disqualified | Eliminated in Stage 1 |

**Monthly forced deployment calculation:**
At ₹25,346 Cr AUM (Bandhan), assume 1% monthly SIP inflow = ~₹253 Cr/month. With 65% in small cap = ₹164 Cr/month needs to go into small cap stocks. If average stock has ₹2,000 Cr market cap and SEBI allows 10% max stake = ₹200 Cr max per stock. To deploy ₹164 Cr/month, need to add ₹2-5 Cr per existing position (80 stocks) OR open new positions. Compared to Edelweiss at ₹5,952 Cr: ~₹60 Cr/month into small cap = much more manageable.

**ER impact on 10Y SIP corpus:**

For a ₹20K/month SIP at 20% gross CAGR over 10 years:
- At 0.34% ER (Bandhan): Net 19.66% → Corpus ~₹82.1 lakh
- At 0.64% ER (DSP): Net 19.36% → Corpus ~₹80.6 lakh → ₹1.5 lakh less
- At 0.85% ER (Sundaram): Net 19.15% → Corpus ~₹79.5 lakh → ₹2.6 lakh less

The ER gap between Bandhan (0.34%) and Sundaram (0.85%) is 0.51%. Over 10 years this costs ~₹2.6 lakh on a ₹24 lakh principal.

**Module 4 Scorecard — Sub-dimensions:**

| Sub-dimension | Weight | Scoring guide |
|---------------|--------|---------------|
| Expense Ratio | High | ≤0.45% = 5, 0.45-0.65% = 4, 0.65-0.80% = 3, 0.80-1.0% = 2 |
| AUM (sweet spot) | Critical | ₹2,000-12,000 Cr = 5; ₹12,000-20,000 Cr = 3; ₹20,000-30,000 Cr = 2 |
| Monthly forced deployment risk | High | Calculate and score vs peers |
| Exit load structure | Medium | 1% for < 12 months = standard; no exit load = concern (redemption pressure) |
| True all-in cost (ER + turnover impact) | Medium | Turnover × illiquidity premium |
| AUM trajectory (growing or stable?) | Low | Fast-growing at large AUM = escalating constraint |

### Module 5 — Fund Manager Quality (15%)

**Additional considerations for Small Cap:**

| Dimension | FlexiCap importance | Small Cap importance |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Tenure on fund | High | High |
| Philosophy clarity | High | High |
| 2018 stress test (manager was there?) | Medium | **Critical** |
| Research team depth | Medium | **Critical** — small caps need original research; no sell-side coverage |
| AUM management decisions | Medium | **Very High** — did the manager voluntarily close/gate the fund as AUM got large? |
| Stock discovery capability | Medium | **Critical** — the alpha in small cap comes from finding companies before consensus |
| SEBI regulatory record | High | High |

**Key question for each manager:** When the fund's AUM grew rapidly (2021-2023), did the manager:
(a) Migrate toward safer, larger small-caps (losing alpha but protecting capital), or
(b) Maintain the same 400th-600th rank stock selection (preserving alpha but with execution risk), or
(c) Voluntarily gate/close the fund to new SIPs (investor-first decision)?

Any fund that closed SIPs voluntarily (like Nippon and SBI did at their AUM peaks) scores higher on manager alignment than funds that kept accepting unlimited inflows.
