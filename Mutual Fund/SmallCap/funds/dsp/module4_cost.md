## What Module 4 Is Really Asking

Module 4 answers two questions that most investors skip:

**Question 1 — How much of your return is silently consumed by fund costs every year?**

The expense ratio is deducted from NAV every single day — in bull markets, in bear markets, in flat periods — regardless of performance. It is the most predictable component of long-term investment cost. A fund charging 0.64% has a permanent 0.64% annual headwind baked into every day of your SIP. Compounded over a decade on a growing corpus, that number — even though it looks small — is material.

**Question 2 — Is the fund's AUM so large that the manager can no longer run the strategy that generated the historical track record?**

At ₹17,906 Cr, DSP Small Cap is the 2nd largest small cap fund in India. The alpha Vinit Sambre generated when the fund was ₹1,000–5,000 Cr was earned in a fundamentally different operating environment. Position sizing, entry and exit execution, portfolio diversification, and cash management all change with scale. Every new ₹5,000 Cr of inflow changes what is and is not possible.

For DSP Small Cap, both questions produce nuanced answers: the expense ratio is **not the cheapest** in the peer group but sits just below the category average, while the AUM is a **genuine and growing constraint** that is already visibly shaping portfolio construction — 81 stocks, 8.38% cash, the voluntary closure history — all are direct consequences of scale.

DSP charges its regular plan investors 1.67% — within regulatory limits, but only modestly below the ~1.83% ceiling. This is different from BOI FlexiCap (which charged 35% below its SEBI maximum) or Edelweiss FlexiCap. DSP is not demonstrating unusual cost restraint at the regular plan level.

**However, for Direct Plan investors, the regulatory framing is different.** Direct plan TER = Regular Plan TER − Distribution Commission. SEBI does not set a separate Direct plan cap — it flows automatically. DSP's 0.64% Direct plan is reasonable under this structure. The 1.03% distribution commission is what distributors earn for selling the Regular plan.

## The AUM Story — ₹17,906 Cr and Three Times Sambre Closed the Door

**DSP Small Cap's AUM history is the most interesting in the entire small cap category** — because Vinit Sambre and DSP AMC have proactively restricted inflows multiple times to protect existing investors, sacrificing fee revenue to do so.

| Date | Action | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|

| **Sep 2018** | Reopened SIP/STP only | IL&FS crash had corrected NAV 33%; new capital at lower prices less dilutive |

**What this history communicates:**

1. **Sambre and DSP AMC have proven willingness to sacrifice fee revenue to protect investors.** Closing the fund from Feb 2017 to Sep 2018 (SIP) and Apr 2020 (lumpsum) meant foregoing months of inflow-driven AUM growth that generates ER revenue. This is rare in India's mutual fund industry, where AMCs almost universally prefer to keep accepting flows.

2. **The COVID-low reopening (Apr 2020) was strategically excellent.** Investors who entered April 2020 at ~₹38–40 NAV are at ₹226 NAV as of May 2026 — a 465–496% return in 6 years. By accepting capital precisely when markets were most distressed, DSP gave new investors an exceptional entry point.

3. **The current open stance is a different kind of AUM management.** Rather than another closure, Sambre has adapted portfolio construction — more stocks (88→81 after consolidation attempt), higher cash buffer (8.38%), wider sector diversification. This is portfolio-level rather than gate-level AUM management.

**At DSP's ₹17,906 Cr, a 1% position requires ₹179 Cr deployed into the small cap universe.** Building this cleanly — without moving the price against yourself — takes 10–15 days of patient accumulation. During that window, other market participants notice the buying pressure, prices drift up, and the entry cost is higher than the initial thesis price. This is the market impact cost that never appears in the stated ER but is real.

**The entry problem in concrete terms:**

A small cap company with ₹3,000 Cr market cap and 40% free float (~₹1,200 Cr institutionally available) trades approximately ₹15–30 Cr/day. Safe institutional buying is 5–10% of daily volume to avoid moving the market:

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Target position (1% DSP) | ₹179 Cr |
| Safe daily buying rate | ₹1.5–3.0 Cr/day |
| Days to build position | 60–120 days |
| Estimated price drift from own buying | 3–8% |
| Effective entry premium over thesis price | ₹5–14 Cr per position |

Over a 60–120 day entry window, other participants notice the consistent order flow. What Sambre identified at ₹100/share may cost ₹106–108 to complete. This invisible friction is why **low turnover (19–24%) is Sambre's most important cost management tool** — the entry/exit friction is a one-time charge per position, not an annual drag. At 19% turnover, each position is replaced only once every ~5 years.
