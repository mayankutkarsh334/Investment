## What Module 4 Is Really Asking

Module 4 answers two questions most investors skip:

**Question 1 — How much of your return is silently consumed by fund costs every year?**

The expense ratio is deducted from NAV every single day — in bull markets, in bear markets, in flat periods — regardless of performance. It is the single most predictable and permanent component of long-term investment cost. A fund charging 0.34% has a 0.34% annual headwind baked into every day of your SIP. Compounded over a decade on a growing corpus, even small fractions of a percent are material.

On this question, **Bandhan is the undisputed winner of the entire shortlist** — at 0.34%, it charges roughly *half* of DSP (0.64%), a third of Axis (0.91%), and less than every peer by a meaningful margin.

**Question 2 — Is the AUM so large, or growing so fast, that the manager can no longer run the strategy that built the track record?**

Here the answer is far less comfortable. Bandhan's AUM **doubled from ₹10,000 Cr to ₹20,000 Cr in roughly ten months** (Apr 2025 → Feb 2026) and now sits at ₹25,346 Cr — the 4th largest of the eight shortlisted funds, squarely in the "constrained" ₹20–30K Cr zone. And it got there with the gates wide open during a performance-chasing surge — the exact opposite of DSP's disciplined, investor-first closures.

For Bandhan, the two questions pull violently in opposite directions. **Cost = best-in-class. AUM trajectory + execution discipline = among the weakest in the study.** Module 4 is where Bandhan's headline strength and its structural weakness collide most directly. The three lines that define this module:

- *Cheapest fund to own (0.34% ER = 5/5)*
- *Most expensive to run among boutique peers (51.96% turnover + ₹253 Cr positions = worst hidden friction)*
- *Zero demonstrated flow discipline (doubled AUM in a year; the anti-DSP)*

Bandhan turns over half its portfolio annually, meaning it pays the same toll **2.5× more often**. The result: Bandhan incurs the highest annual market-impact friction of any of the boutique-quality comparison funds, despite its rock-bottom stated ER.

## Portfolio Turnover — The Hidden Cost That Erodes the ER Edge

**At 51.96% annual turnover, Bandhan has the highest portfolio churn of all 8 shortlisted funds.** This creates a structural hidden cost that does not appear in the 0.34% ER but does appear in NAV outcomes.

**The rotation philosophy at work:** Bandhan's portfolio manager appears to run a rotation-oriented strategy — entering undervalued positions and exiting as they re-rate, rather than DSP's buy-and-hold compounding approach. In a small-cap context, this means paying market-impact costs at exit as well as entry, on a portfolio that is half-renewed every two years.

**Market-impact compounding — the core arithmetic:**

| Scenario | Holding Period | Per-Position Round-Trip Cost | Annual Market Impact Cost |
|----------|---------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| DSP (20% turnover) | ~5 years | ~5% market impact per trade | **~1% of position/year** |
| Invesco (40% turnover) | ~2.5 years | Same | **~2% of position/year** |
| **Bandhan (52% turnover)** | **~2 years** | **Same** | **~2.5% of position/year** |
| Hypothetical (100% turnover) | ~1 year | Same | **~5% of position/year** |

At ₹253 Cr average position with 52% annual turnover, Bandhan pays market-impact friction at 2.5× the rate of DSP per unit of invested capital. This is the hidden engine eating into the 0.34% cost advantage.
