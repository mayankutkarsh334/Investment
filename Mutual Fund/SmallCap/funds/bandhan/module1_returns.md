## ⚠️ Critical Flag 1: The ER Anomaly — Four Sources, Four Numbers

The expense ratio for Bandhan Small Cap Fund Direct Growth is reported inconsistently across platforms — and the spread is more extreme than any other fund studied in this project.

| Source | ER Reported | Data Date | Notes |
|--------|------------|-----------|-------|
| **Tickertape Screener CSV** | **0.34%** | May 21, 2026 | Used in Stage 1/2 screening; passed the ≤1.0% filter easily |

| **Dhan / FundScreener** | **0.49–0.51%** | May 2026 | Yet another value |
| **Value Research Online** | **0.80%** | May 23, 2026 | AMFI official disclosure — most reliable |
| **AdvisorKhoj** | **0.80%** | April 30, 2026 | AMFI disclosure |

**Resolution:** The canonical ER is **0.80%** from VRO and AdvisorKhoj, both sourced from AMFI's official Total Expense Ratio (TER) disclosures. The 0.34% on Tickertape and INDmoney is almost certainly a stale data feed — a temporary TER reduction that was not sustained, or a feed update lag that has not been corrected.

**This is a larger discrepancy than seen in Invesco (0.40% TT vs 0.66% VRO = 0.26% gap). For Bandhan, the gap is 0.46 percentage points (0.34% vs 0.80%).** In a fund charging 0.80%, reporting 0.34% on screeners is a 57% understatement of the true cost.

**Investment implication at 0.80% ER:**
- Bandhan's 0.34% screener ER made it appear to be the cheapest fund in the shortlist by a wide margin
- At the canonical 0.80%, Bandhan ranks **6th of 8** on ER (behind DSP 0.64%, Invesco 0.66%, BOI 0.49%, Kuvera/Dhan 0.48–0.51% suggesting 0.50% true ER for others)
- The "ER = 5/5" advantage from screening evaporates; the true cost structure is middle-of-pack
- This is analyzed in full in Module 4; for Module 1 returns analysis, the key point is that **stated returns are net of this 0.80% ER**, so return figures are accurately stated — but the cost context changes materially

**Why this matters for return comparison:** All CAGR figures reported by MFAPI are NAV-based (post-ER). The fund has delivered its returns *after* the 0.80% annual fee has been deducted. The 30.95% 3Y CAGR is real money in investors' hands, not a pre-fee figure. The ER anomaly matters most in Module 4 (cost analysis) and for the final comparative score.

## ⚠️ Critical Flag 3: The COVID Crash Paradox — Resolved

**The fund's -18.1% COVID crash severity (vs -36% to -49% for other SC funds) is not a deception. Here is the full explanation.**

```mermaid
xychart-beta
    title "COVID Crash Severity — Bandhan vs Peers (Peak-to-Trough)"
    x-axis ["Bandhan", "Invesco", "DSP", "Category Avg"]
    y-axis "Max Drawdown % (COVID)" -55 --> 0
    bar [-18.1, -36.1, -49.1, -45]
```

| Event | Date | Bandhan NAV | Notes |
|-------|------|-------------|-------|
| Fund inception | Feb 26, 2020 | ₹10.00 | Fund is 1 day old |
| COVID crash trough | Mar 23, 2020 | **₹8.19** | **-18.1% from inception NAV** |
| Year-end recovery | Dec 31, 2020 | ₹15.15 | +84.9% from trough |

**Why only -18.1% when mature SC funds fell 36–49%?**

Three interlocking factors:

1. **Fund was 4 weeks old with minimal AUM (~₹100–200 Cr estimated):** At launch, the portfolio was mostly in cash/T-bills as the manager was still deploying capital into SC stocks. With predominantly cash holdings, the fund barely fell when SC stocks crashed.

2. **Structural: Any portfolio has a build-up period.** A newly launched small cap fund cannot immediately deploy 65% into illiquid SC stocks at scale. The early weeks involve gradual deployment. COVID hit before deployment was complete — essentially protecting the fund by accident.

3. **The -18.1% is genuine NAV-based math:** The NAV went from ₹10.00 to ₹8.19 — a real loss for investors who bought at inception. But the scale was limited because the portfolio wasn't fully invested yet.

**This is NOT the same as saying the fund managed risk better than peers during COVID.** There was no active risk management decision by Bhaskar — the portfolio simply had less market exposure because it was newly launched. ## SIP Implication

Bandhan Small Cap Fund is the most paradoxical fund in the 8-fund shortlist. Every raw return metric puts it at #1 — highest 3Y and 5Y CAGR, highest alpha, best max drawdown, best Sharpe. And yet the score (3.50/5) is lower than Invesco (3.72) despite better numbers.

The reason is structural. The headline numbers are built on:
1. An inception point that coincided with the COVID crash trough — the best possible starting date
2. A manager change in January 2023 that completely transformed the fund's quality — meaning only 3 years of current management exist
3. A measurement window (2023–2025) that happened to be one of the strongest small cap periods in history

What the data cannot tell you:
- How Gunwani would have performed in 2018, when the Nifty SC250 fell 26% and quality funds like DSP still fell 25%
- Whether the 10.87%/year Gunwani alpha is durable skill or recency
- Whether the 2022 -4.59% under Bhaskar (worse than benchmark) is a one-off or predictive of the fund's underlying quality

**What the data does credibly show:**
- Gunwani's 3-year track record at this fund is exceptional — 10.87%/year average alpha across bull and weak market years
- The 2025 protection (+6.15% vs benchmark -6.01%) demonstrates genuine downside management at scale (₹25K Cr AUM)
- The 2024 outperformance (+18.74% above benchmark in an up market) is not pure defensiveness — this is active stock selection generating alpha in a broad bull year
- The PE of 18.53 (41% below category average) provides a valuation cushion that is absent in Invesco (PE 43.43) and DSP (PE 29.54)

**For a ₹20K/month SIP starting today:**
- The fund is near ATH — consistent with the sector recovery; neutral for SIP entry
- Base-case 10Y SIP projection: ₹24L → ₹58–73L (16–20% XIRR), contingent on Gunwani continuity
- The critical watch item: will Gunwani stay, and does he replicate 2023–2025 alpha through a genuine small cap crash year?
- The single biggest risk: if Gunwani exits (as Bhaskar did), the replacement manager could easily revert to Bhaskar-era performance (-3.75% alpha/year) — erasing the alpha premium

**What a Bandhan SIP investor is essentially betting on: Manish Gunwani's ability to sustain 10%+ annual alpha over a 10-year horizon through at least one major small cap crash.** That is a high conviction bet on a 3-year track record. It may be justified — but it requires Module 5 analysis of Gunwani's pre-Bandhan history to validate.

**Recommended holding period:** Minimum 7 years. This is a small cap fund — 1-3 year horizons will produce volatile outcomes. SIP discipline through a future crash year (which the fund has not yet experienced at scale) will be the defining test of the investment thesis.
